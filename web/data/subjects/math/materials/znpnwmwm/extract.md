20. In the figure, $ACD$ and $BCE$ are two straight lines. $CE = 10$ cm,  
$DE = 8$ cm, $\angle ABC = 72^\circ$ and $\angle BAC = 60^\circ$.  
(a) Find $\angle DCE$.  
(b) If $\angle CDE$ is an acute angle, find $\angle CDE$.  

(a) $\angle ACB + 60^\circ + 72^\circ = 180^\circ$, $\angle DCE = \angle ACB$  
$\angle ACB = 48^\circ$  
$\angle DCE = 48^\circ$  

(b) $\frac{10}{\sin \angle CDE} = \frac{8}{\sin 48^\circ}$  
$\angle CDE = 68.3^\circ$

21. In the figure, $ABC$ is a straight line and $BD = CD$. $AD = 16$ cm,  
$\angle BAD = 55^\circ$ and $\angle BCD = 61^\circ$.  
(a) Find $\angle ADB$.  
(b) Find the length of $AB$.  

(a) $\angle DBC = \angle DCB = 61^\circ$  
$\angle ABD = 119^\circ$  
$\angle ABD + 55^\circ + \angle ADB = 180^\circ$  
$\angle ADB = 6^\circ$  

(b) $\frac{AB}{\sin 6^\circ} = \frac{16}{\sin 119^\circ}$  
$AB = 1.91$ cm

22. In the figure, $P$, $Q$, $R$ and $S$ are points on a circle. $PR = 19$ cm,  
$PS = 11$ cm and $\angle PQR = 67^\circ$.  
(a) Find $\angle SPR$.  
Explain (b) Is $\triangle PRS$ an isosceles triangle? Explain your answer.  

(a) $\angle PSR = \angle PQR = 67^\circ$  
$\frac{11}{\sin \angle PRS} = \frac{19}{\sin 67^\circ}$  
$\angle PRS = 32.2^\circ$  
$\angle SPR + 32.2^\circ + 67^\circ = 180^\circ$  
$\angle SPR = 80.8^\circ$  

(b) $\because \angle SPR \ne \angle SRP$ (by calc)  
$\therefore \text{No}$
