$$

20. In the figure, $ACD$ and $BCE$ are two straight lines. $CE = 10$ cm,  
$DE = 8$ cm, $\angle ABC = 72^\circ$ and $\angle BAC = 60^\circ$.  
(a) Find $\angle DCE$.  
(b) If $\angle CDE$ is an acute angle, find $\angle CDE$.

20. (a) In $\triangle ABC$,  
$$
\angle BAC + \angle ABC + \angle ACB = 180^\circ
$$  
$$
60^\circ + 72^\circ + \angle ACB = 180^\circ
$$  
$$
\angle ACB = 48^\circ
$$  
$$
\angle DCE = \angle ACB
$$  
$$
= 48^\circ
$$  

(b) In $\triangle CDE$, by the sine formula,  
$$
\frac{10 \text{ cm}}{\sin \angle CDE} = \frac{8 \text{ cm}}{\sin 48^\circ}
$$  
$$
\sin \angle CDE = \frac{10 \sin 48^\circ}{8}
$$  
$$
\angle CDE = 68.3^\circ, \text{ cor. to } 3 \text{ sig. fig.}
$$  

21. In the figure, $ABC$ is a straight line and $BD = CD$. $AD = 16$ cm,  
$\angle BAD = 55^\circ$ and $\angle BCD = 61^\circ$.  
(a) Find $\angle ADB$.  
(b) Find the length of $AB$.

21. (a) $\because BD = CD$  
$\therefore \angle CBD = \angle BCD = 61^\circ$  
In $\triangle ABD$,  
$$
\angle ADB + \angle BAD = \angle CBD
$$  
$$
\angle ADB + 55^\circ = 61^\circ
$$  
$$
\angle ADB = 6^\circ
$$  

(b) $\angle ABD + \angle CBD = 180^\circ$  
$$
\angle ABD + 61^\circ = 180^\circ
$$  
$$
\angle ABD = 119^\circ
$$  
In $\triangle ABD$, by the sine formula,  
$$
\frac{AB}{\sin 6^\circ} = \frac{16 \text{ cm}}{\sin 119^\circ}
$$  
$$
AB = \frac{16 \sin 6^\circ}{\sin 119^\circ} \text{ cm}
$$  
$$
= 1.91 \text{ cm}, \text{ cor. to } 3 \text{ sig. fig.}
$$  

22. In the figure, $P$, $Q$, $R$ and $S$ are points on a circle. $PR = 19$ cm,  
$PS = 11$ cm and $\angle PQR = 67^\circ$.  
(a) Find $\angle SPR$.  
Explain (b) Is $\triangle PRS$ an isosceles triangle? Explain your answer.

22. (a) $\angle PQR + \angle PSR = 180^\circ$  
$$
67^\circ + \angle PSR = 180^\circ
$$  
$$
\angle PSR = 113^\circ
$$  
In $\triangle PRS$, by the sine formula,  
$$
\frac{11 \text{ cm}}{\sin \angle PRS} = \frac{19 \text{ cm}}{\sin 113^\circ}
$$  
$$
\sin \angle PRS = \frac{11 \sin 113^\circ}{19}
$$  
$$
\angle PRS = 32.203^\circ, \text{ cor. to } 5 \text{ sig. fig.}
$$  

$$
\angle PRS + \angle SPR + \angle PSR = 180^\circ
$$  
$$
32.203^\circ + 113^\circ + \angle SPR = 180^\circ
$$  
$$
\angle SPR = 34.8^\circ, \text{ cor. to } 3 \text{ sig. fig.}
$$  

(b) The three angles in $\triangle PRS$ are all different.  
$\therefore \triangle PRS$ is not an isosceles triangle.
