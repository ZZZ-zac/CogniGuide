29. (a) $\triangle ABC$ is an isosceles triangle.  
$\therefore \quad BD = BC = \frac{1}{2} \times 4\,\text{cm} = 2\,\text{cm}$  

In $\triangle ABD$, by Pythagoras’ theorem,  
$AB^2 = AD^2 + BD^2$  
$AD = \sqrt{3^2 - 2^2}\,\text{cm}$  
$= \sqrt{5}\,\text{cm}$  
$AE = \frac{1}{2} AD$  
$= \frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}\,\text{cm}$  
$ED = AE = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}\,\text{cm}$  

In $\triangle BDE$, by Pythagoras’ theorem,  
$BE^2 = BD^2 + ED^2$  
$BE = \sqrt{2^2 + \left(\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)^2}\,\text{cm}$  
$= \sqrt{\frac{21}{4}}\,\text{cm}$  
$= \frac{\sqrt{21}}{2}\,\text{cm}$

(c) In $\triangle ABE$, by the sine formula,  
$\frac{AE}{\sin\theta} = \frac{BE}{\sin\angle BAE}$  
$\sin\theta = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{2} \cdot \frac{\sin\angle BAE}{BE}$  
$\sin\angle BAE = \frac{BD}{AB} = \frac{2}{3}$  
$\sin\theta = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{2} \cdot \frac{\frac{2}{3}}{\frac{\sqrt{21}}{2}} = \frac{2\sqrt{5}}{3} \cdot \frac{2}{\sqrt{21}} = \frac{2\sqrt{105}}{63}$
